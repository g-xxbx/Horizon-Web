## [Karpathy's 'Pelican on a Bicycle' Benchmark Sparks AI Evaluation Debate](https://twitter.com/karpathy/status/2083749667410727319) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Andrej Karpathy tweeted about the 'pelican on a bicycle' benchmark, igniting a debate about whether AI models need new qualitative benchmarks that test physical world understanding beyond simple image generation. The discussion quickly expanded across the AI community, with Karpathy himself posting a follow-up about the benchmark's limitations. This matters because it points to a shift in how AI models are evaluated, from image quality toward physical world understanding and reasoning. The outcome could influence how developers and researchers design future benchmarks and what capabilities they prioritize in frontier models. The benchmark originated with developer Simon Willison in late 2024, using the simple prompt 'Generate an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle.' Karpathy's follow-up tweet noted that the SVG pelican has a ground truth that can be eyeballed in one glance, whereas a playable game is a much harder benchmark to eyeball at scale.

hackernews · delichon · Aug 2, 04:05 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49140998)

**Background**: The 'pelican on a bicycle' is an informal benchmark for large language models that tests their ability to produce valid, visually coherent Scalable Vector Graphics code for a physically impossible scene. It was created by Simon Willison in late 2024 as an alternative to traditional text-based benchmarks, which often fail to capture qualitative aspects of visual and spatial reasoning. Andrej Karpathy is a prominent AI researcher known for his work on deep learning and his role as a former director of AI at Tesla, so his commentary carries significant weight in the community.

## [Open letters split AI industry over open-weight model policy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/2/open-letters/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Simon Willison's article summarizes recent open letters on AI development. A Microsoft-led letter, 'Open Weights and American AI Leadership,' signed by 235 companies including NVIDIA, Amazon, and OpenAI, defends open-weight models and distillation. This reveals a major industry divide over AI policy, as major tech firms push back against potential U.S. restrictions on open-weight models. The letters could influence AI regulation, competition, and safety debates. Anthropic notably declined to sign and published its own position warning of authoritarian misuse of open weights and calling for crackdowns on industrial-scale distillation. A separate 'Pacing the Frontier' letter, signed by 1,324 employees of frontier AI labs, urged the U.S. to support international governance tools for paced AI development.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 2, 04:16

**Background**: Open-weight models are AI models whose trained parameters are publicly released, enabling anyone to download, run, and modify them. This contrasts with closed models that are accessible only via APIs. The Microsoft letter argues that relying solely on closed models creates single points of failure and weakens competition, while critics raise safety and misuse concerns.
