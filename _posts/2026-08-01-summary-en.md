## [OpenAI's Astra solves ten decade-old math problems for under $2,000 each](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/1/ten-advances-in-mathematics/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On August 1, 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal version of Astra, its next major model, produced new results on ten long-open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, with no prior progress for at least a decade. The company says each solution cost less than $2,000 in GPT-5.6 Sol token prices, and it released Lean 4 formalizations, a paper, and a reasoning-walkthrough PDF. This is significant because it follows Anthropic's similar claim that Claude/Mythos Preview discovered cryptographic weaknesses, suggesting frontier AI systems can now produce genuine research-level mathematical results at remarkably low cost. It could push mathematics toward 'big mathematics' — large-scale human-AI collaboration where AI handles much of the technical work — while prompting urgent scrutiny from the mathematical community. OpenAI has not disclosed how many problems it attempted without success, so the overall success rate remains unknown. The openai/ten-proofs GitHub repository contains Lean 4 certified proofs, and OpenAI explicitly states that while the mathematical arguments were generated by the model, humans organized and formalized them.

**Background**: Lean 4 is an interactive proof assistant that lets mathematicians write proofs in a machine-checkable formal language, making it possible to verify complex arguments automatically. The problems addressed include high-dimensional sphere packing, the existence of non-sofic groups, a counterexample to Connes's rigidity conjecture, arithmetic circuit lower bounds, quantum parallel repetition, the hardness of the closest vector problem, and multicolor Ramsey numbers. These results recall earlier 'Deep Blue' moments and Terence Tao's vision of 'big mathematics,' in which complex tasks are split between humans and AI.

## [NetBSD 11.0 Released with Fast MicroVM Kernel and NPF Firewall Gains](https://blog.netbsd.org/tnf/entry/netbsd_11_0_released) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NetBSD 11.0 was released, introducing a new MICROVM kernel for x86 that boots in about 10 ms, along with NPF firewall improvements such as layer 2 and user/group filtering. This major release strengthens NetBSD's position in lightweight virtualization and embedded scenarios, while the firewall enhancements benefit server and desktop users. It also sparks community discussion about the adoption and relevance of BSD systems. The MICROVM kernel supports both i386 and amd64, leveraging PVH boot, VirtIO MMIO, and multiple kernel optimizations. The NPF firewall now includes layer 2 filtering and rules based on users and user groups.

**Background**: NetBSD is a free, open-source Unix-like operating system known for its portability and clean design. NPF is NetBSD's BSD-licensed stateful packet filter, comparable to Linux's iptables. The MICROVM kernel is designed for extremely fast booting in virtual machines such as QEMU.

## [Stateless MCP 2.0 Revives Interest, Inspires Two New Tools](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/31/stateless-mcp/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Simon Willison reports that the 2026-07-28 MCP 2.0 specification, also called Stateless MCP, removes session state so tools can be called in a single HTTP request. He built two new tools around it this week: mcp-explorer and datasette-mcp. This is the largest MCP spec change since the protocol launched, and it greatly lowers the complexity of building clients and servers. Because stateless MCP tools are easier to audit and control, smaller on-device models can drive them well, making MCP a more viable alternative to giving agents raw shell and internet access. Under the stateless model, a tool call is sent as one POST with MCP-Protocol-Version, Mcp-Method, and Mcp-Name headers, replacing the old two-step initialize-then-call flow with Mcp-Session-Id. The change also improves scalability, since servers no longer need to maintain session state or route same-session requests to the same backend.

rss · Simon Willison · Jul 31, 23:13

**Background**: MCP is the Model Context Protocol introduced by Anthropic in November 2024 as a standard way to expose tools to LLM-powered agent frameworks. It saw huge interest through 2025, then was somewhat eclipsed by Anthropic's Skills when it became clear that an agent harness with a terminal and curl could do much of what MCP did. The 2026-07-28 specification, first released as a release candidate on May 21, 2026, introduces a stateless protocol core along with extensions framework, tasks, MCP apps, and authorization hardening.

## [VLM Benchmarks Reward Empty Reports, Erase Clinical Terms](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vcipzz/vlms_can_score_well_on_benchmarks_while_silently/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new study shows that vision-language model benchmarks for chest X-ray report generation reward repetitive, clinically empty outputs, silently erasing rare but meaningful medical terms and introducing hallucinated bias. The authors propose a framework to quantify this term erasure and measure introduced bias. This exposes a critical flaw in how medical VLMs are evaluated, meaning models can score well while producing reports with little clinical utility. It could drive the development of more clinically meaningful evaluation metrics and safer AI for radiology. The paper focuses on radiology report generation (RRG) for chest X-rays, showing that high benchmark scores can coexist with erasure of rare clinical terms and the introduction of biased terms. The proposed framework is intended to explicitly measure term erasure and bias rather than relying solely on existing aggregate metrics.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/ade17_in · Aug 1, 09:27

**Background**: Vision-language models (VLMs) are multimodal models that process both images and text, often used for tasks like automated radiology report generation. Radiology report generation aims to automate the writing of medical reports from imaging, but evaluation metrics can be misleading if they reward common patterns rather than clinically important details. Term erasure refers to the phenomenon where models omit rare but meaningful terms, while bias introduction refers to adding terms or patterns that reflect hallucinations or dataset biases.

## [Inside Go Networks: Symmetry Study Uncovers Unexpected Representations](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vcrki2/how_symmetric_are_the_insides_of_a_go_network_r/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The author of the open-source Go program KataGo published a detailed interpretability study on how superhuman Go-playing neural networks handle board symmetry. Using stochastic 8-fold data augmentation, the study examines whether the network learns orientation-invariant representations or memorizes per-orientation concepts, and reports one unexpected finding. This study advances ML interpretability by revealing how deep models internalize domain symmetries, which can inform architecture design and training strategies for symmetric domains beyond Go. It also provides a rare, accessible look inside a superhuman game-playing network from a leading practitioner. The study focuses on KataGo, a strong open-source Go engine, and does not enforce symmetry in the model architecture; only stochastic 8-fold data augmentation is applied during training. The write-up was largely AI-generated but with detailed human direction and feedback, and the code is linked from the post.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/icosaplex · Aug 1, 16:18

**Background**: The game of Go is played on a square board, and its rules are invariant under rotations and reflections — the symmetries of the square (the dihedral group D4). Many game-playing neural networks, including KataGo, do not bake these symmetries into the network architecture, but instead rely on data augmentation: each training sample is randomly transformed to one of the 8 possible board orientations. Prior machine learning theory has examined when such augmentation can capture the full benefits of symmetry, and how invariant or equivariant representations emerge.

The buyer consortium consists of Saudi Arabia's PIF, Silver Lake, and Affinity Partners. PIF has been steadily increasing its stakes in gaming companies and has previously completed full acquisitions of developers such as Scopely and Niantic.
